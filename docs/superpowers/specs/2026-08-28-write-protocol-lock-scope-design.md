# Write Protocol Lock Scope Redesign

Status: design, 2026-08-28, revision 3 (final design round). Target branch: `rewrite/single-context-impl` (evaluated at 18eb3289). All `file:line` citations refer to that commit unless prefixed with `master:`. The maintainer approved the "API and behavior delta list" at the end of this document on 2026-08-28; revision 3 introduces no consumer-visible change beyond that list, and any implementation discovery that needs one must extend the list explicitly rather than absorb it.

Revision 3 changes, in one paragraph: the terminal predicate is gated on one rule everywhere (the `IsStructuralRoute` field), and the executor's unattached scalar arm now sets that field for declared-structural properties, resolving the route 3 contradiction toward correctness; the currency line is scoped to lifecycle appearance (a chain compiled without a lifecycle does not commit once the context has one), which removes the same-chain re-fire that plain `AddService` calls would otherwise cause; the verify step's scoping and the `TryClaimDiscovered` signature are specified implementably, keeping the return-false contract; the null rule's dominance over the currency line and the raw-SPI edge-publication widening are stated as such; the ordering-attribute diagnostic moves to registration time; the reconcile-entry citations are corrected to the real line numbers; and the API and behavior delta list gains the classifier removal (now approved, with its three tests named), the newly reachable guard, the narrowed-write release-descent behavior change, and the blast-radius confinement citations.

Revision 2 summary (kept for history): terminal null rule and lifecycle write-through arm; state currency at commit; declared-type classification via a precomputed metadata flag; worklist verify step; bounded executor retry; per-write context fields as the single terminal mechanism.

## Problem

Two deadlocks were reproduced against the branch, both regressions versus master:

- **Repro A** (`src/Namotion.Interceptor.Tracking.Tests/GateChainDeadlockRepro.cs`): two contexts, each with a third-party `IWriteInterceptor` that performs a structural write into the other context after `next()`. Deadlocks on the branch (both threads park holding their own context's topology gate, each wanting the other's), passes on master.
- **Repro B** (`src/Namotion.Interceptor.Tracking.Tests/MonitorAbbaRepro.cs`): one lifecycle-free context, two subjects, an interceptor cross-writing between them after `next()`. Deadlocks on the branch (unordered ABBA on the two per-subject attachment monitors), passes on master.

Both were reproduced in this worktree before any change (40 s timeouts), and both pass under the prototype described in "Prototype evidence" below (about 50 ms).

## 1. Verdict on the diagnosis

**Partially right.** The diagnosis has two claims; one is confirmed, one is refuted.

**Confirmed: the executor's lock scope is wider than any guarantee requires.** `InterceptorExecutor.SetStructuralPropertyValue` enters the lifecycle gate at `src/Namotion.Interceptor/Interceptors/InterceptorExecutor.cs:275`, the attachment monitor at `InterceptorExecutor.cs:278`, and then runs the entire interceptor chain inside both (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:285` calling `WriteStructuralValue`, which executes the chain at `InterceptorExecutor.cs:329`). The lifecycle-free arm holds the monitor alone across the chain (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:265-271`). That scope covers third-party interceptor code, property-change subscriber dispatch, and derived cascades on the unwind, none of which the ordering guarantee needs. It is also confirmed that pinning does not require the locks: the pinned-state property from `c78328af` is a single volatile read threaded from the routing decision into the chain resolution (`PinState` and the `FromState` overloads, `src/Namotion.Interceptor/InterceptorSubjectContext.cs:193-196` and the `ExecuteInterceptedWrite(ContextState, ...)` overload at `:239`), and nothing about that read depends on any monitor being held. The conflation is real.

**Refuted: fixing the executor alone does not fix Repro A, so this is not only an executor lock-scope error.** `LifecycleInterceptor.WriteProperty` takes the gate itself inside the chain and holds it across `next()` (`src/Namotion.Interceptor.Tracking/Lifecycle/LifecycleInterceptor.cs:130-157`, `next` at `:143`). Every first-party write interceptor orders before the lifecycle (`PropertyValueEqualityCheckHandler.cs:10` `[RunsFirst]`, `DerivedPropertyChangeHandler.cs:20,25`, `PropertyChangeInterceptor.cs:22-23`, `SubjectTransactionInterceptor.cs:13`, `ValidationInterceptor.cs:14`), but a third-party interceptor registered without ordering attributes keeps insertion order (`InterceptorSubjectContext.cs:346-350` comment on `ComputeServices`) and therefore lands **after** the lifecycle, inside the gate held across `next()`. `OwnershipGraph.cs:494-497` documents exactly that placement. Repro A's interceptors are registered after `WithFullPropertyTracking`, so removing only the executor's outer hold still leaves their post-`next` cross-write running under the gate, and the deadlock stands. The design below therefore changes both the executor scope and the chain position of the lifecycle.

**Also confirmed as the real constraint the wide scope was papering over:** the monitor-across-chain is what today prevents a detach-and-reattach-elsewhere from interleaving with an in-flight commit. If the subject leaves context C and joins context D mid-chain, C's stale chain can commit a backing value that D's attach seeding already read past, leaving an untracked edge in D. Master had this window and admitted it (the reread comment in `master:src/Namotion.Interceptor.Tracking/Lifecycle/LifecycleInterceptor.cs`, in `WriteProperty`: "context.NewValue may differ from the backing store if another thread overwrote the property between our next() call and lock acquisition"). Any narrowing of the scope must replace that protection with something that closes the window rather than reopening it. That replacement is the terminal-time commit predicate described next.

## The third shape

Master was deadlock-free but racy (validate after write, reread to converge). The branch is race-free on the covered paths but deadlock-prone (locks span user code). The third shape separates the two jobs the wide locks were doing:

1. **Topology atomicity** (claim before write, reconcile after write, no topology change interleaving with either) stays with the lifecycle gate, which the lifecycle already takes inside the chain. What changes is that no registered interceptor can run inside it, because the lifecycle becomes the last interceptor in every write chain, so `next()` under the gate is the terminal and nothing else.
2. **Commit-versus-transition atomicity** (a write commits only against the world its chain was resolved for) moves from "hold the monitor across the chain" to a **commit predicate evaluated under the attachment monitor at the terminal**, immediately around the commit. The monitor is held for the commit only, never across user code. A failed predicate re-routes the whole write against the fresh attachment; an unattached subject at commit time commits (the null rule, motivated in section 4a).

### Why this closes master's reread race instead of reintroducing it

With the lifecycle last, the terminal commit of every attached structural write executes inside the lifecycle's gate section (`next()` at `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:143` reaches the terminal directly). Two concurrent writes to the same structural property serialize their entire [claim, commit, reread, reconcile] sections on the gate, so the reread at `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:147-148` cannot observe another thread's commit landing inside this write's section, provided **every** commit path to a declared-structural property is covered by the gate or the monitor. Two paths violated that proviso in revision 1 and are closed here: the narrowed-`TProperty` path (section 4b) and the stale-chain path (section 4c). The reread's remaining job is the one it legitimately has: observing what a normalizing setter actually stored, which is same-thread and deterministic.

## 2. The precise new lock scope, per route

Route classification stays as today: `TProperty` that can contain subjects routes structural (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:213`, backed by `PropertyTypeIndex<TProperty>.CanContainSubjects`, `InterceptorSubjectContext.cs:44`), everything else routes scalar; unattached means the lock-free attachment read returned null.

### The per-write context fields (the single terminal mechanism)

Four internal fields on `PropertyWriteContext<TProperty>`, which already carries per-call chain state (`Terminal` at `src/Namotion.Interceptor/Interceptors/IWriteInterceptor.cs:47`, `Executor` at `:56`, `Revision` at `:62`):

- `IsStructuralRoute` (bool): the terminal runs the commit predicate **exactly when this field is set, and this is the only gating rule; the terminal consults no metadata**. It is set by: the executor's structural branch, always; and the executor's scalar branch's **unattached arm**, when the declared property type is structural (`context.Property.Metadata.CanContainSubjects && !Metadata.IsDerived`, one metadata dictionary lookup, `PropertyReference.cs:20-29`, paid on that arm only). It is never set by the scalar branch's attached arm (covered by the lifecycle's gate section, section 4b, residuals in section 8) and never by the cascade re-entry path (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:341-366`), which targets derived properties and has no retry loop; keeping the cascade permanently off the predicate is what makes an aborted cascade impossible rather than merely unlikely. The scalar branch does have a retry loop (route 4), so its unattached arm can answer an abort.
- `ExpectedAttachedContext`: the context the routing read (null on the unattached arms). Overwritten to null by the lifecycle's write-through arm (section 4a).
- `ChainState`: the pinned `ContextState` the chain was resolved from (null on the unattached arms).
- `AttachmentMoved` (bool): set by the lifecycle's re-route arm or by a failed commit predicate; answered by the setting route's retry loop.

All four are per-attempt: each retry constructs a fresh context, which is also the `AttachmentMoved` reset (an interceptor that calls `next()` twice within one attempt reaches the terminal twice, exactly as today). The constructor consumes the thread-static pending origin (`IWriteInterceptor.cs:121-130`); retries thread the first attempt's `AttemptedOrigin` through an internal constructor instead of consuming again, on all routes.

### The commit predicate (under the attachment monitor, run iff `IsStructuralRoute`)

```
enter monitor
actual = executor.AttachedContext
if actual == null:                                   commit          // the null rule
else if actual != ExpectedAttachedContext:           AttachmentMoved // moved: re-route
else if !chainHasLifecycle && ChainState != null
        && actual.CurrentState != ChainState
        && actual now resolves an ILifecycleInterceptor:
                                                     AttachmentMoved // a lifecycle appeared: re-route
else:                                                commit
exit monitor
```

Commit means today's terminal body under `SyncRoot`: backing write, `IsWritten`, revision increment, origin finalize, `SetWriteState` (`src/Namotion.Interceptor/Cache/WriteInterceptorFactory.cs:19-41`). `chainHasLifecycle` is captured at chain compile time: `WriteInterceptorFactory.Create` receives the interceptor array (`WriteInterceptorFactory.cs:9`) and the partition already scans it for `ILifecycleInterceptor`, so the capture costs one closure allocation per compiled chain, nothing per write. The lifecycle-resolve on the third line runs only on the rare path (lifecycle-free chain, state changed since the pin) and uses the current state's cached service lookup. The currency line is deliberately scoped: it fires only for the delta that carries a correctness obligation, a lifecycle the chain does not contain (section 4c); any other service registration concurrent with an in-flight write leaves the write alone.

Two dominance facts, stated explicitly: the null rule consults neither `ExpectedAttachedContext` nor `ChainState`, so the "current at commit time" property of section 4c is **conditional on the subject being attached at commit**; an unattached commit is governed by the null rule's own ordering argument below. And the predicate governs only routes that set `IsStructuralRoute`; the attached scalar route and the cascade never run it.

The null rule is safe because a commit on an unattached subject is ordered against every future claim by the monitor itself: `TryUpdateAttachment` takes the same monitor (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:113-134`), so a claim either lands before the commit (predicate sees the new context and re-routes) or after it (the claimant's seeding reads the committed value; acquire/release on the monitor gives the visibility). The one hole the null rule would open, release by this thread followed by a cross-thread re-attach to the **same** context before commit, is closed because the lifecycle's write-through arm sets `ExpectedAttachedContext` to null first, so a re-attached subject fails line 2 and re-routes (section 4a).

### Route 1: attached, context has a lifecycle (structural)

Executor (`SetStructuralPropertyValue`): **holds nothing**. Bounded loop (`MaxWriteRouteAttempts`, section 4d):

1. Volatile-read the attached context, pin its `ContextState`, resolve the chain from the pinned state.
2. Construct the write context with `IsStructuralRoute`, `ExpectedAttachedContext = attached`, `ChainState = pinned` (origin threaded on retries).
3. Execute the chain. If `AttachmentMoved`, loop; else return `IsWritten`.

Lifecycle (`WriteProperty`, guaranteed last before the terminal by the partition below): properties that are non-intercepted, derived, or whose **declared type** cannot contain subjects pass through unchanged; the classification is a precomputed metadata flag (section 4b), not the current `metadata.Type.CanContainSubjects<TProperty>()` at `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:108`. Otherwise, after the callback guard (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:117`), three arms on `subject.Executor.AttachedContext`:

- **This context**: `lock (_gate)`, re-check under the gate (attached transitions of this context's subjects all hold the gate: claims and releases require it, `OwnershipGraph.cs:21-24`, and both explicit entry points hold it, `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:273,306`; raw SPI transitions are caught by the terminal predicate, with the one edge-publication widening named in section 8). Then exactly as today: `IsOwned`, `ClaimProposedComponent` (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:138-141`), `next()` (terminal only), and, **only if the terminal committed** (`IsWritten` true and `AttachmentMoved` false), the authoritative reread and `Reconcile` (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:147-148`); the existing `finally` releases the attempt's unused claims either way (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:150-156`), so an aborted attempt leaves no claim residue before the executor retries.
- **Null** (the write-through arm): set `ExpectedAttachedContext = null` and call `next()`. No gate, no claims, no reconcile; the terminal's null rule commits if the subject is still unattached at commit time, and re-routes if any context claimed it meanwhile. This is the same-write release shape (section 4a).
- **Another context**: set `AttachmentMoved`, return without `next()`. The chain belongs to a context the subject has left; the retry runs the current owner's chain.

The gate-section re-check that lands on null exits the gate and takes the write-through arm; landing on another context sets `AttachmentMoved` under the gate.

Held while user code runs on this route: **nothing** for third-party interceptors, subscriber dispatch and derived cascades (all upstream of the lifecycle, so their post-`next` segments run after the gate is released). The gate covers, as today and as on master, the reconcile's user-code surfaces: lifecycle callbacks and events (contractually constrained, `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:48-49,60`, `CallbackReentrancyGuard.cs:59-63`), user collection and dictionary code enumerated by the scanner (master enumerated inside its lock too, `master:LifecycleInterceptor.cs` `FindSubjectsInProperty` under `lock (_attachedSubjects)`), read interceptors during the authoritative reread (also inside master's lock), and the terminal `writeValue` delegate (a registry dynamic property's stored setter can be user code, `src/Namotion.Interceptor.Registry/Abstractions/RegisteredSubjectProperty.cs:204`; branch status quo, listed as a residual in section 8).

### Route 2: attached, lifecycle-free context (structural)

Executor: same bounded loop, holds nothing. Chain: the context's compiled chain, no lifecycle in it (`chainHasLifecycle` false). Terminal: the commit predicate. A detach mid-chain commits via the null rule (with notification, since `IsWritten` becomes true and the chain's interceptors see it); a move to another context re-routes; a `WithLifecycle()` registration since the pin re-routes via the scoped currency line (section 4c). This is what fixes Repro B.

### Route 3: unattached (structural or scalar `TProperty`)

The unattached arms stop bypassing the terminal: both route through the zero-interceptor chain (`UninterceptedChain<TProperty>`, `InterceptorExecutor.cs:180-184`) instead of the raw `writeValue` call at `InterceptorExecutor.cs:313-321`, restoring `SyncRoot`, the commit revision and `SetWriteState`. Whether the predicate runs is decided **in the executor, before dispatch**, by the field rule above: the structural branch's unattached arm always sets `IsStructuralRoute` (with `ExpectedAttachedContext = null`); the scalar branch's unattached arm sets it when the declared type is structural and not derived, which is the metadata consult priced in the field list (that arm already pays an `_attachedContext` read and a chain dispatch; a true-scalar unattached write sets nothing and keeps the plain `SyncRoot`-only terminal, since seeding never reads scalar properties, `OwnershipGraph.cs:179-185`). The declared-structural consult on the scalar arm is what closes the narrowed-unattached lost-edge window (section 5, finding 3): the commit either precedes the claim and is seen by seeding via monitor ordering, or fails line 2 and re-routes into the attached protocol, where section 4b's classification validates it. The alternative, skipping the lookup and documenting the narrowed-unattached window as a residual, was considered and rejected: it fails the stated "no race windows" bar, and the lookup lands on a route no named benchmark measures; nothing in the code makes the lookup unacceptable there (the arm is already a dictionary-dispatch away from the generated setter's fast path, and the lookup is the same `Subject.Properties` access the lifecycle performs on every intercepted write at `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:106-107`).

### Route 4: scalar `TProperty`, attached

Unchanged locks: no gate, no monitor, chain from a fresh state read, plain terminal (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:218-231`). Two additions: the executor's scalar branch gets the same bounded `AttachmentMoved` loop (a narrowed structural write can be re-routed by the lifecycle's arms, and the unattached arm's predicate can abort), and retries thread the consumed origin. A narrowed write on an attached subject runs the lifecycle's full gate section (declared-type classification, section 4b), which covers it against all lifecycle-mediated transitions; the raw-SPI and late-registration residuals specific to this route are in section 8 and are accepted, not covered.

### The chain partition

`CreateWriteInterceptorFunction` (`InterceptorSubjectContext.cs:288-294`) partitions the resolver's output at chain compile time: all non-`ILifecycleInterceptor` write interceptors in resolved order, then the `ILifecycleInterceptor` instances. This applies only to write-chain compilation; `GetServices<IWriteInterceptor>()` keeps the resolver order (and `WritePipelineOrderTests.cs:16-56` stays accurate: its expected arrays already end with `LifecycleInterceptor`, because every first-party interceptor orders before it, so the partition is a no-op for first-party-only registrations). Attributes are not used for the partition because they are class-level and would also move the lifecycle's `ILifecycleHandler` slot, breaking the handler seam where `SourceMonitor` and `HostedServiceHandler` run `[RunsAfter]` it (`docs/design/tracking-lifecycle.md`, "Handler Order").

An ordering attribute that would place an `IWriteInterceptor` after an `ILifecycleInterceptor` is answered by **throwing at registration time**, in the mutation path next to the existing contract validation (`ValidateSingletonContracts`, `InterceptorSubjectContext.cs:104-130`): the check rejects exactly three shapes: (a) a registering `IWriteInterceptor` declares `[RunsAfter]` naming a registered `ILifecycleInterceptor`'s type; (b) a registering `ILifecycleInterceptor` finds an existing `IWriteInterceptor` declaring `[RunsAfter]` naming it; (c) a registering `ILifecycleInterceptor` declares `[RunsBefore]` naming a registered `IWriteInterceptor`. `[RunsBefore]` on a **write interceptor** naming a lifecycle is never rejected: it requests exactly the position the partition guarantees, two first-party interceptors declare it (`PropertyChangeInterceptor.cs:23`, `DerivedPropertyChangeHandler.cs:20`), and `WithPropertyChangeSubscriptions()` registers one of them standalone with no lifecycle (`src/Namotion.Interceptor.Tracking/InterceptorSubjectContextExtensions.cs:83-91`), so the legal composition `context.WithPropertyChangeSubscriptions().WithLifecycle()` must keep working when the lifecycle registers second. The diagnostic message names both services. Registration time rather than chain compile time because compilation is lazy and would surface the diagnostic from an unrelated first write (`InterceptorSubjectContext.cs:277-286`), where the consumer cannot act on it. The impossibility argument: any interceptor downstream of the lifecycle executes inside the topology gate (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:130-157` spans `next()`), where any blocking operation can deadlock against an opposing gate holder; Repro A is the reproduction. No in-repo consumer declares such an ordering (verified by grep over `IWriteInterceptor` implementers).

### Interface cleanup

`ILifecycleInterceptor.EnterStructuralWriteGate`/`ExitStructuralWriteGate` (`src/Namotion.Interceptor/Interceptors/ILifecycleInterceptor.cs:40-46`) lose their only production caller (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:275,291`). Remove both members. Full blast radius: the Core SPI members, the two **public** methods on the public sealed `LifecycleInterceptor` in Tracking (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:87-96`), both assemblies' `PublicApi` verified snapshots, and eleven test files whose fake lifecycles implement them (grep `EnterStructuralWriteGate` under `src/*Tests`). Approved as part of the delta list.

## 3. The four hard constraints

**(1) Pinned state (`c78328af`).** The routing decision and the chain still resolve from one pinned `ContextState`; the executor no longer makes a separate routing decision at all, so the two cannot disagree by construction. What changes is the fate of a pin that goes stale before commit: today (and in revision 1) it commits ("a lifecycle registered after the pin is invisible to this write as a whole", `InterceptorExecutor.cs:262-266`); this design re-routes exactly the stale pins that lack a lifecycle the context now has, and only for attached commits (the null rule does not evaluate currency). The commit `c78328af` closed the window where routing and chain disagreed with **each other**; this design keeps that closed and additionally closes the window where both agree with a world that has since gained the lock authority. Its test changes accordingly (section 7), and the semantics retirement is on the approved delta list.

**(2) The ordering guarantee** (`IInterceptorExecutor.cs:77-93`): an attach or detach racing a structural write orders against it rather than failing it; only a persistent conflict throws, before the backing field is written.

- Claim-before-write is untouched: `ClaimProposedComponent` runs before `next()` under the gate (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:141-143`), and the foreign-context throw happens before any backing mutation (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:181-187`).
- A racing transition orders through one of: (a) lifecycle-mediated transitions of an attached subject wait on the gate the write section holds; (b) a release lands and the commit proceeds unattached under the null rule, with notification delivered; when the release lands before the lifecycle's gate re-check, no edges publish (the write-through arm makes no claims and runs no reconcile, matching the reconcile-entry-check semantics at `StructuralReconciler.cs:42-55`); when a **raw SPI** detach lands after the gate re-check, the reread and reconcile still run and edges **do** publish for a subject whose executor reads unattached, because `Reconcile`'s guard is graph ownership, which a raw `TryUpdateAttachment` never clears (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:113-134`); that cell is the widened residual named in section 8, not an ordering success; (c) a claim or move lands and the write re-routes through the new owner's chain and protocol. None of these throws.
- The throw semantics: a re-routed attempt that finds a proposed subject genuinely claimed by another context throws from `ClaimProposedComponent`, a persistent conflict at that claim's evaluation time; the inter-attempt claim-cycle nuance is a named residual in section 8.
- The bound: after `MaxWriteRouteAttempts` re-routes of one logical write, the executor throws a diagnostic `InvalidOperationException` (section 4d), with today's alternative for the triggering shape being a silent unvalidated write-through (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:120-126`). Approved as part of the delta list.
- Empirical: the ordered-not-failed stress tests (`StructuralWriteLockOrderTests.cs:38-119`) pass unchanged under the prototype.

**(3) The documented lock order** (gate, then attachment monitor, then SyncRoot). Scoped claim, twice over: **on the structural write protocol, the library itself acquires locks only in the documented order**: gate then monitor (claims; the terminal predicate under the gate on route 1), monitor then SyncRoot (the checking terminal), gate then SyncRoot (the reread through the read terminal, `ReadInterceptorFactory.cs:22`); a failed predicate releases the monitor before the retry re-routes, so no path holds the monitor while acquiring a gate. User delegates are outside the claim: the terminal runs `innerWriteValue` inside monitor and SyncRoot (`WriteInterceptorFactory.cs:21,53`), which is user code for hand-written subjects and registry dynamic properties, and such a delegate acquiring a gate would invert the order; that is the stored-setter residual of section 8, already present on the branch (which runs the same delegate under gate, monitor and SyncRoot today) and narrower here. Outside the write protocol, `InterceptorExecutor.AddProperties` is unchanged: its lifecycle-free arm publishes under the monitor (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:391-398`), and `SubjectPropertyRegistration.Publish` acquires no gate (`SubjectPropertyRegistration.cs:88-110`), so it remains a leaf acquisition as documented.

**(4) The full suite passes.** Prototype evidence in section 8: 3,460 tests, four deterministic failures, each pinning the old lock scope, all rewritten by section 7. This is not claimed as "no other behavior moved": section 7 enumerates the tests that keep passing while their coverage shifts, and the API and behavior delta list at the end enumerates the visible deltas. Constraint 5 (no new `InternalsVisibleTo`) holds: every boundary crossed already exists (`Namotion.Interceptor.csproj:15-18` grants Core internals to Tracking).

## 4. The mechanisms in detail

### 4a. The null rule and the write-through arm

The repo documents and tests a third-party interceptor releasing the writing parent during the write (`docs/design/tracking-lifecycle.md`, "Callback Contract"; `DownstreamWriteInterceptorReleaseTests.cs:19-67`, whose interceptor releases **before** calling `next`, `:95-107`). Under the partition that interceptor runs upstream, so the lifecycle meets an already-released subject. Revision 1 answered with abort-and-retry, which committed the value through the retried zero-interceptor chain: final state correct (the test's asserts all pass, which is how the v1 prototype passed it), but `IsWritten` stayed false on the chain that contained `PropertyChangeInterceptor`, so the change event (`PropertyChangeInterceptor.cs:166`) and the derived recalculation (`DerivedPropertyChangeHandler.cs:159`) were silently lost, a regression against both master and the branch that no current assertion catches.

The write-through arm restores parity in one pass: the lifecycle observes null, sets `ExpectedAttachedContext = null`, and calls `next()`; the terminal's null rule commits; `IsWritten` is true on the original chain; notification and recalculation fire. No claims are made and no reconcile runs, which is today's semantics for a released writing parent (the reconcile-entry check, `StructuralReconciler.cs:42-55`, reached the same outcome from inside the gate). A context mismatch at the lifecycle has two causes, this thread's own upstream interceptor and a cross-thread transition, and the arms distinguish them by what the current attachment is rather than by guessing.

Why `ExpectedAttachedContext` must be overwritten to null: if it stayed C, a cross-thread re-attach to C between the release and the commit would satisfy line 2 and commit a value that C's re-attach seeding already read past (a lost edge on C). With expected null, that interleaving fails the predicate and re-routes through C's full protocol. The `WhenAttachmentChangesBetweenEntryAndTerminal_ThenStructuralWriteStillCommits` test (`StructuralWriteTests.cs:147-170`, transition interceptor at `:15-26`) is the same-thread detach shape and keeps its single-pass commit semantics under the null rule.

### 4b. Declared-type classification in the lifecycle

Verified: `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:108` calls `metadata.Type.CanContainSubjects<TProperty>()`, and that overload short-circuits on `TProperty` alone for primitives, `decimal`, `string`, `DateTime`, `DateTimeOffset`, `TimeSpan` and `Guid` before consulting the declared type (`SubjectPropertyTypeExtensions.cs:31-45`). Consequence today: `IInterceptorExecutor.SetPropertyValue<int>` against a property with declared type `object` (structural per `OwnershipGraph.cs:59`) commits through the scalar route with no gate, no monitor, and **no reconcile ever**: a subject previously held by that property keeps its committed edges while the backing store holds an int, a pre-existing staleness defect shared with master (same generic short-circuit there). A concurrent gate-holding structural write's reread can also observe such a commit mid-section, which is master's reread race surviving into revision 1. The narrowing is a supported public shape (`HandWrittenSubjectWriteTests.cs` drives it; `IInterceptorExecutor.cs:85-89` documents it).

Fix, verified for cost: the lifecycle classifies on the declared type only, via a `CanContainSubjects` flag precomputed at `SubjectPropertyMetadata` construction (public readonly record struct, `SubjectPropertyMetadata.cs:6-16`; one public bool property, snapshot update, approved) from `Type.CanContainSubjects()`. `LifecycleInterceptor.WriteProperty` already loads the metadata before the check (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:106-107`), so the check becomes field reads: for primitive `TProperty` this replaces a JIT-folded constant with one predictable branch on an already-loaded struct (~zero delta); for reference-typed `TProperty` it removes the `ConcurrentDictionary` lookup the fallback performs today (`SubjectPropertyTypeExtensions.cs:44,52-57`). `OwnershipGraph.IsStructural` (`OwnershipGraph.cs:57-60`) adopts the same flag. With this, every write to a declared-structural property on an attached subject, whatever its `TProperty`, runs the full gate section: claim (an int value yields no occurrences and claims nothing), commit, reread, reconcile (which now correctly releases the edges of a subject overwritten by a scalar value). The executor's routing and the terminal keep their `TProperty`-based fast paths unchanged. The behavior deltas this creates for narrowed writes are on the approved list.

### 4c. State currency at commit, scoped to lifecycle appearance

A chain resolved from a `ContextState` pinned before a `WithLifecycle()` registration contains no lifecycle; without the currency line it passes line 2 and commits with no gate while a concurrent post-registration write holds the gate, reopening the reread window at the registration boundary (today the executor's monitor-across-chain happens to serialize this; `ContextConcurrencyTests.cs:30-60` drives concurrent registration generally). The currency line closes exactly that: an **attached** structural commit whose chain lacks a lifecycle re-routes when the context's current state resolves one, and the fresh pin sees it.

The scoping matters (round 2 finding B8): an unscoped `CurrentState != ChainState` check would re-route, and therefore **re-run the same context's chain**, on any `AddService` concurrent with an in-flight structural write, with no attachment change at all; live-context registration during startup is real (`src/HomeBlaze/HomeBlaze.Services/RootManager.cs:47,55,94`). Scoped to lifecycle appearance, a plain service registration never disturbs an in-flight write (the late interceptor is seen by the next write, the pre-existing semantics for every other late registration), and the one re-route that can re-run a same-context chain is the `WithLifecycle` registration itself, which the singleton contract makes a once-per-context event (`ValidateSingletonContracts`, `InterceptorSubjectContext.cs:130`): at most one extra chain execution per in-flight write racing that one-time registration, named in section 4e and in the residuals.

### 4d. Retry bounds and livelock

The retry-before-the-chain argument in the current design doc does not transfer to a retry around the chain: the chain itself can cause retries on the same thread. The null rule removes the dominant same-thread source (an interceptor that releases the subject causes no retry at all); what remains requires an interceptor that detaches **and re-attaches** the subject (a direct swap is rejected, `InterceptorExecutor.cs:121-125`, so ping-pong means deliberate detach-plus-attach cycles inside `WriteProperty` on every attempt), or the once-per-context lifecycle registration (section 4c). The executor's loop is bounded: `MaxWriteRouteAttempts = 100`, matching the repo's existing bound for user-code-driven instability (`DerivedPropertyChangeHandler.MaxStabilizationIterations = 100`, `DerivedPropertyChangeHandler.cs:32-35`). On exhaustion the executor throws `InvalidOperationException` naming the subject and the attempt count. Defense: today the ping-pong shape does not loop, it silently commits through a context that never validated or reconciled the value (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:120-126`), which is corruption; one hundred genuine cross-thread transitions of one subject inside one write is not a plausible workload; and the current design doc already declines starvation-freedom for this protocol.

### 4e. Interceptor re-execution across attempts

Third-party interceptors carry no `IsWritten`-guard or idempotence obligation (`IWriteInterceptor.cs:8-21`), so attempts must be rare and meaningful. An attempt is re-run only when (i) the subject's attachment genuinely changed between routing and commit, in which case the retry runs the chain of the **new** attachment (a C-to-null race commits in one pass under the null rule with no retry; a null-to-C race re-routes a zero-interceptor attempt into C's chain, firing C's interceptors once; a C-to-null-to-D race fires C's interceptors on the aborted attempt and D's on the committing one, and only D's chain publishes, since dispatch is `IsWritten`-guarded in first-party interceptors, `PropertyChangeInterceptor.cs:166,215`, `DerivedPropertyChangeHandler.cs:159`), or (ii) the context gained its lifecycle mid-write (section 4c), which re-runs the **same** context's chain exactly once more. Re-firing the same context's interceptors therefore requires the subject to leave and re-enter that context, or the once-per-context lifecycle registration; both are bounded. No new obligation is imposed: an aborted attempt followed by a re-routed attempt is observationally equivalent to the caller losing the race and re-invoking the setter after the transition, which interceptors already must tolerate.

## 5. The three related findings

**Claim/discovery race: fix, in this design.** Confirmed as described: `DiscoverComponent` reads getters of unattached subjects with no synchronization (`OwnershipGraph.cs:437`), the unattached write validates nothing (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:313-321`), and a foreign-context subject written into the component between discovery and `TryClaimDiscovered` (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:181`, `:354`) surfaces as a throw from `AttachEdge` mid-publication (`AttachTraversal.cs:56-60`), leaving claimed-but-unowned members and committed baselines (`OwnershipGraph.cs:192`) that `DetachSubjectFromContext` does not clean beyond the root's own claim (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:314-319`). Two mechanisms close it:

1. The commit predicate on the unattached arms (route 3) closes the post-claim window: an unattached-routed write carries `ExpectedAttachedContext = null`, so a commit attempted after the subject was claimed finds `actual` non-null and unequal to expected, sets `AttachmentMoved`, and re-routes into the attached protocol, where the attacher's gate blocks it until the descent completes.
2. A **worklist** verify step inside `TryClaimDiscovered`, run under the gate the caller already holds, closes the discovery-to-claim window. Specification: `TryClaimDiscovered` gains the discovery's visited set as a parameter, `bool TryClaimDiscovered(List<IInterceptorSubject> unattached, HashSet<IInterceptorSubject> visited, IInterceptorSubject? explicitRoot, SubjectAttachmentAnchorKind rootAnchor)` (`OwnershipGraph.cs:470`). After the initial claim pass it processes each newly claimed subject **exactly once**: reread its structural getters and collect occurrences; an occurrence attached to this context or already in `visited` is skipped; an occurrence attached to a foreign context releases every claim this operation made and **returns false**, preserving the method's existing contract (`OwnershipGraph.cs:466-469`) so each caller keeps its own distinct exception message (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:184-186` for the write path, `:356-357` for the explicit attach); an unattached occurrence is claimed, appended to the caller's `unattached` list (so `ReleaseUnusedClaims` and the rollback path cover it), added to `visited`, and appended to the worklist. Call-site restructuring, which round 2 showed is required: `ClaimComponentForRoot` already has `visited` in scope at its claim call (rented `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:349`, claim at `:354`, returned `:362`) and only changes the argument list; `ClaimProposedComponent` must widen its scratch scope, because today `visited` is returned to the pool, **which clears it** (`LifecycleScratch.cs:119-124`), in a `finally` at `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:176-179` that runs before the claim at `:181`; the restructured method rents `visited`, runs `DiscoverComponent` and `TryClaimDiscovered` inside one `try`, and returns the set in a `finally` that covers both.

Termination: the verify pass terminates exactly when `DiscoverComponent` terminates, because it processes each visited subject at most once over the same getter surface the discovery walk reads; a getter that returns a fresh subject on every read already makes the discovery walk itself diverge (each fresh subject is new to `visited` and is pushed, `OwnershipGraph.cs:407-458`), so the verify pass introduces no new non-termination class. Sufficiency: after a subject is claimed, no new value can be committed into it unobserved (mechanism 1 plus the gate), so the single post-claim reread is authoritative for cross-thread effects; same-thread effects (getters invoked by the verify pass run on the attacher's thread, which holds the reentrant gate, `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:44`) fold into the single pass.

Cost, priced: one additional structural-getter pass over the **newly claimed** subjects of each claim operation, skipped entirely when the discovered-unattached set is empty. `ReplaceSingleChildReference` (~2.68 us) claims one fresh subject per write, so it pays one extra pass over that subject's structural properties, order 50-150 ns by getter-invocation count on the benchmark model; the benchmark comparison in section 7 gates it.

**Unattached writes bypass the terminal: fix, in this design.** Route 3 restores `SyncRoot`, the commit revision and `SetWriteState` via the zero-interceptor chain, matching the unattached scalar route (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:223`) and master. Cost: uncontended `SyncRoot` plus bookkeeping, plus the monitor and one metadata lookup only for declared-structural properties, on a route no named benchmark measures.

**The narrowed-`TProperty` claim: the doc is false today; this design makes the attached half true and the unattached half ordered.** The claim at `IInterceptorExecutor.cs:87-88` ("the lifecycle still takes its own gate inside the chain, so ownership stays consistent") fails today in two ways: for short-circuited `TProperty` (section 4b: the lifecycle never engages, no gate, no reconcile, stale edges), and for the unattached race (a narrowed write routed unattached commits with no revalidation while an attach seeds past it, a silent lost edge). Section 4b closes the first on attached subjects; route 3's executor-side declared-structural consult closes the second. The doc sentence is rewritten to the retry semantics, in all three copies: `IInterceptorExecutor.cs:85-89`, `docs/design/tracking-lifecycle.md` "The Write Protocol", and the `PropertyTypeIndex` comment at `InterceptorSubjectContext.cs:36-42`.

## 6. Alternatives considered and rejected

**(a) The cheap option: keep the scope, refuse a second gate.** Track gate ownership per thread; a structural write that would take context B's gate while holding context A's throws instead of deadlocking. Rejected. It converts both repros from hangs into new runtime exceptions for patterns that work on master, a silent consumer-visible constraint. It does nothing for Repro B without additionally refusing the monitor, which would break the innocent lifecycle-free cross-subject pattern entirely. It treats the symptom and forfeits the internal-fix goal, and the prototype shows the internal fix is itself small.

**(b) Master's shape: `next()` first, lock and reread after.** Rejected: surrenders both halves of what the branch bought, validation before the backing write and a deterministic reread.

**(c) Global lock ordering across contexts.** Rejected as impossible in principle: the second context is discovered inside user code mid-chain, so the lock set is unknowable up front, and Repro B's monitors are per-subject and consumer-ordered, so no static rank exists.

**(d) Narrow the executor's hold but keep the lifecycle mid-chain.** Rejected: fixes Repro B only; Repro A's interceptor still runs inside the gate held across `next()` (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:130-157`, insertion-order placement per `InterceptorSubjectContext.cs:346-350`).

**(e) Split the lifecycle's gate hold (claim under gate, release during `next()`, retake for reconcile).** Rejected: reintroduces master's window (another thread's commit lands between this write's commit and reconcile), lets detach-plus-reattach interleave the two sections wholesale, and exposes provisional claims to other contexts as spurious "owned by C" conflicts, violating "only a persistent conflict throws".

**(f) Revision 1's terminal rule: abort-and-retry on every mismatch, including null (the v1 prototype shape).** Superseded within this design. It passes the whole suite, but it silently drops change notification and derived recalculation for the documented release-during-write shape (section 4a; the covering test asserts final state only, so it stayed green), re-runs upstream interceptors for that common shape, and its livelock argument fails for chain-caused same-thread transitions (section 4d).

## 8. Prototype evidence, residual risks, and what could not be determined

**Prototype.** A throwaway prototype lives uncommitted in this worktree, every hunk marked `PROTOTYPE (throwaway)`, in `InterceptorExecutor.cs`, `WriteInterceptorFactory.cs`, `IWriteInterceptor.cs`, `InterceptorSubjectContext.cs`, `LifecycleInterceptor.cs`. It implements the **revision 1** core: chain partition, lock-free executor loop (unbounded), abort-on-any-mismatch terminal under the monitor (no null rule, no currency line), lifecycle staleness abort, unattached writes through the zero-chain with an unconditional check. It is evidence, not the implementation; the implementation is re-derived from this document. Results: both repros pass in ~50 ms; full unit suite 3,460 tests with exactly the four expected scope-pinning failures; concurrency category stable across repeated runs; the two long-running registry stress tests show no standalone timing change (17-19 s vs 16 s baseline; an initial 48 s reading was parallel-suite interference, reproduced as such). The revision 2 and 3 amendments are design-only and each is pinned by a named test in section 7; the implementer should expect `DownstreamWriteInterceptorReleaseTests` to pass even if the null rule is implemented wrongly, which is why its new notification assertion is not optional.

**Residual constraints and windows, each named:**

- User code invoked from inside the gate by the reconcile itself (collection/dictionary implementations, dictionary-key `Equals`, read interceptors during the reread) must not structurally write into a different context. Identical exposure existed on master inside its lock.
- A registry dynamic property's stored setter executes at the terminal under monitor and SyncRoot (and the gate on route 1); a stored setter that acquires a lifecycle gate inverts the lock order (constraint 3's scoping). Branch status quo (today it runs under gate, monitor and SyncRoot), narrower here, wider than master. Document on `AddProperty`.
- **Raw-SPI edge publication, widened from same-thread to cross-thread**: a raw `TryUpdateAttachment` detach landing after the lifecycle's gate re-check lets the commit proceed via the null rule with `IsWritten` true, so the reread and `Reconcile` run and publish edges for a subject whose executor reads unattached, because `Reconcile` guards on graph ownership (`StructuralReconciler.cs:42-55`), which a raw detach never clears (`InterceptorExecutor.cs:113-134`). Today this cell is reachable only from the writing thread (the monitor-across-chain blocks cross-thread raw transitions mid-write); this design makes it reachable from any thread. Same hole class (raw SPI bypassing the lifecycle for an owned subject corrupts the graph today too), wider reach; named here instead of claimed away.
- **Inter-attempt claim cycle**: an aborted attempt releases its provisional claims (`ReleaseUnusedClaims` in the `finally`, `LifecycleInterceptor.cs:150-156`), so a competing context can claim a proposed subject between attempts and turn a would-have-succeeded write into the persistent-conflict exception on the retry (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:184-186`). Reachable causes: a genuine attachment transition of the written subject, and the once-per-context lifecycle-appearance re-route (section 4c). The outcome is a true conflict at evaluation time, but the window is created by the release-and-reclaim cycle, so it is a residual, not a non-event.
- Narrowing residuals on the **attached** scalar route: raw-SPI transitions racing a narrowed attached write are ordered only for lifecycle-mediated transitions (the gate), and a narrowed attached write whose chain predates a `WithLifecycle` registration commits without the gate (the scalar route carries no `ChainState`). Both require the caller to have explicitly narrowed `TProperty` below the declared type, the documented opt-out.
- A getter that returns a fresh subject per read defeats once-per-subject verification and already diverges the discovery walk itself (`OwnershipGraph.cs:407-458`); best-effort claiming, seed-time throw reachable, same user-error class as the derived handler's stabilization bound.
- The write retry loop is livelock-free for lifecycle-mediated causes and bounded at `MaxWriteRouteAttempts` for everything else; neither loop is starvation-free under adversarial churn, unchanged in kind from the current design doc's caveat.
- The lifecycle-appearance re-route (section 4c) re-runs one same-context chain per in-flight write racing the one-time `WithLifecycle` registration; interceptor side effects for that attempt fire twice.

**Not determined, and the experiment that settles each:**

- Whether any external consumer registers an `IWriteInterceptor` ordered after the lifecycle, by attribute (now a registration-time diagnostic) or by insertion (now executed upstream). In-repo: none. Externally unknowable; PR behavior note.
- Whether the one-time connectors flake is the known one: 50x on the unmodified branch under full-suite load.
- The exact perf deltas, above all `ReplaceSingleChildReference` under the verify pass and `SetScalarAttached` under the classification swap: the benchmark comparison is the gate, with the pinned-CPU recipe.
- Whether `TryGetAttachment` callers tolerate the monitor's new short hold windows at commit time (`LifecycleInterceptor.cs:276,309` are cold-path; re-grep after rebase).
- Whether any test or consumer observes the retired invisible-late-lifecycle semantics outside the rewritten `c78328af` test: grep for `PinState`/`FromState` consumers after rebase.
