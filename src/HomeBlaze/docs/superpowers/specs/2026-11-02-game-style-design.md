# Game style, people and emotes

A pull request on top of #876 that gives the game one consistent look, lets players choose how they look, adds emotes and makes rendering cheaper. Whether it is merged is decided after it is built.

## Goals

- One art direction for every object: low poly shapes in a warm pastel palette, soft sun shadows, corner shading and thin dark outlines with faint drawn lines on surfaces ("style E" of the style lab, a mix of diorama and comic).
- Players are low poly people that match the trees and animals, and each player picks a person and a shirt color.
- Players can clap, sit and jump, and everyone nearby sees it.
- The page renders only as much as the device and the situation need.

Not goals: more than one art style to choose from, realistic rendering, new object types.

## Look

### Surfaces

- `HomeBlaze.Game.Buildings` replaces its photo textures (`bricks.jpg`, `concrete.jpg`, `wood.jpg`) with drawn pastel textures in the same files' roles: flat pastel fill with faint darker lines for mortar, plank seams and concrete joints. They are PNG files generated by a script in the repository, our own work released under CC0; `wwwroot/textures/LICENSE.md` says so and names the script. The texture scales in `WallTextures` change only if the new drawings need it.
- The core's ground texture becomes a pastel grass fill with the meter grid, generated by the same script, replacing `grass.jpg`.
- The sky and fog take the warm diorama tone (`#f3e6d3`), the hemispheric light a slightly lower intensity, the sun a warm tint.
- Trees and animals keep their models; they are already low poly and vertex colored.

### Shadows

- The sun casts real shadows with a percentage closer filtered shadow map. The shadow frustum follows the camera target and covers the visible area around the own player (about 120 tiles across), so its resolution does not spread over the whole view radius.
- Every entity mesh casts a shadow; the ground and boxes receive them. The blob disc under players is removed.
- Labels never cast or receive shadows.

### Post-processing

- A geometry buffer renders depth and normals once per frame. An outline pass draws thin ink lines where depth or normals jump: 55 % strength, 1.5 pixel width, ink 60 % of the way from warm brown to near black (the values chosen in the style lab).
- Corner shading (SSAO2) reads the same geometry buffer instead of its own prepass.
- The first pass of the chain renders the scene with 4x multisampling; a light sharpen (edge amount 0.35) runs last. FXAA is not used, because it blurs the outlines at lower resolutions.
- Labels and faded objects stay out of the geometry buffer, so names get no outline and a wall faded for the sightline leaves no ghost outline.
- Light bands and tilt-shift are not part of the style.

### Quality

A dropdown in the game panel offers Low, Medium, High and Auto. The choice is stored per browser in `localStorage` (it depends on the device, not the player) and defaults to Auto.

| Preset | Resolution | Frame cap | Corner shading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 1x | 30 FPS | off |
| Medium | 1.5x, at most the device pixel ratio | 60 FPS | half resolution |
| High | 2x, at most the device pixel ratio | 60 FPS | full resolution |

- Outlines, drawn lines and shadows stay on in every preset, so the game looks the same everywhere, only sharper or softer.
- Auto starts with Low on a touch device (coarse pointer) and Medium elsewhere, and drops one level after the frame time stays above the cap's budget for five seconds. It never raises the level by itself during a session.

### Rendering only when needed

- While the tab is hidden, the render loop stops; it resumes when the tab shows again. Voice and the server connection keep running, and view updates keep being applied, so the scene is current when rendering resumes.
- While the page is visible but the window has no focus, the frame cap drops to 10 FPS.

## People

### Assets

- Six people from Quaternius' "Animated Men Characters" and "Animated Women Characters" (February 2019, CC0): Male Casual, Male Shirt, Male Long Sleeve, Female Casual, Female Alternative, Female Tank Top, the flat shaded variants.
- They are converted once from FBX to binary glTF by a script in the repository that runs three.js' FBX loader and glTF exporter in a headless browser, turns the materials matte (the exporter writes Phong materials half metallic) and undoes one of the two color space conversions the loader and exporter apply. The script keeps only the animations the game uses: Idle, Walk, Clapping, Sitting and Jump.
- They live in the core under `wwwroot/models/people/` with a `LICENSE.md` that names the source, the license and the conversion. `Casual_2.glb` is removed.

### Player settings

- `GamePlayer.Character` (configuration, string): the file name of the person without extension, or null for the default.
- `GamePlayer.Color` (configuration, string): a `#rrggbb` shirt color, or null for the default.
- The defaults derive from the client id like today's color: `GameColors.FromId` for the color and the same hash modulo six for the person, so players look different without choosing. A value that is not one of the six people or not a valid color falls back to the default.
- Both are saved with the player, like `Facing`, and can be written from anywhere (subject browser, MCP); the game does not refuse them.

### Edit dialog

- The pencil next to the own player in the Nearby list opens "Edit me", replacing "Change my name": the name, a shirt color picker and a row of six person previews (small static images generated from the models by the same script).
- Save renames the client when the name changed and writes `Character` and `Color` on the own player through an internal method of the game that the page calls, like moving. The name may stay empty when only the look changes; an anonymous client then stays anonymous.

### View and browser

- `GameViewPayload` carries the player's `Character` and its resolved color. The browser loads each person file once, instantiates it per player and recolors the material named `Shirt`, as it recolors `LightBrown` today.
- The walk speed ratio is tuned for the new walk cycle so the feet keep pace with one tile per step.

## Emotes

- The panel gets an "Emotes" section with Clap (C), Sit (V) and Jump (B). The keys work like the voice keys: not while a dialog or text field has focus.
- The page calls an internal method of the game, like a step; operations from MCP or other clients cannot trigger an emote.
- The simulation stores the emote on the player entity with a sequence number, so the same emote twice in a row plays twice. Clap and Jump end after their animation length; Sit lasts until the player's next step.
- The view payload carries the emote and its sequence; the browser plays Clap and Jump once and returns to idle, and holds the last frame of Sitting until the emote ends.
- A step during Clap or Jump cancels it. Emotes are not saved and are cleared when the client disconnects.

## Code structure

- `game.js` owns the scene setup, so lights, shadows, the post-processing chain, quality presets and the render loop pause live there, split into a new `game-render.js` module for the quality and pipeline code if `game.js` grows past what reads well.
- `game-entities.js` keeps building entities; it registers shadow casters and keeps labels and faded meshes out of the geometry buffer through a small interface the render module provides.
- Server side: `GamePlayer` gains the two properties, `PlayerEntity` the appearance and emote fields, `GameViewPayloads` the new payload fields, `Game.Players.cs` the edit and emote methods, `GamePage.Panel.cs` the emote section and the quality dropdown, and `GameNameDialog` becomes `GamePlayerDialog`.

## Testing

- Unit tests: default person and color per client id, fallback for invalid values, emote state (sequence, end after the animation length, Sit until the next step, cleared on disconnect), payload fields.
- E2E tests: the quality dropdown persists across a reload; the edit dialog changes person and color and both survive a reload; an emote on one page shows on another page's player (asserted through the canvas data attributes the tests already use); the render loop pauses while the tab is hidden.
- The existing E2E tests keep passing, including the one that checks a prefilled dialog.
- A before and after screenshot of the demo world goes into the pull request.

## Risks

- Phones: shadows and outlines cost two extra geometry passes. Low keeps both, so Low on a weak phone is the case to check by hand; if it is too slow, Low drops shadows to the old blob discs.
- Skinned people in the geometry buffer and the shadow map must use their bones, or outlines and shadows stand in the bind pose; checked in the E2E screenshot.
- The new people are about 1 MB each before trimming; the trimmed files should stay under 400 KB each.

## Documentation

- The game README describes the look, the quality presets, the render pause, the people and the emotes.
- The abstractions README tells object authors that surfaces should use flat pastel colors or drawn textures and that the outline and shading come from the core.
